## Two primitives

Every flow on Corridor is one of two things:

- **Collection:** local currency comes in through an anchor and is credited to the customer's USDC balance on the hub.
- **Payout:** the customer's balance on Stellar goes out to a spoke, where an anchor pays local currency.

A cross-border payment is a collection followed by a payout. The customer's balance on the hub sits between them, so the two halves can happen at different times, in different amounts, and through different anchors.

## Why a hub, and why this one

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="N, not N²" icon="network">
    A bilateral network of six regions needs fifteen links. A hub needs six. Every anchor added reaches every customer and every other region on day one.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Adding a corridor is configuration" icon="plug-zap">
    Anchors speak the same SEPs, so adding one is a home domain and an asset rather than an integration. A corridor is just a currency pair: `GHS-HKD` works the moment a GHS collector and an HKD payer exist.
  </Card>
  <Card title="No bridge on the common path" icon="git-merge">
    A Stellar anchor settles on the hub itself. The fiat spoke touches the hub directly, so the usual payout is `ledger.reserve → partner.payout` with nothing in between.
  </Card>
  <Card title="One place to hold and reconcile" icon="scan-search">
    Customers keep one balance instead of a float in each country, and every movement is settled, held and reconciled in one ledger.
  </Card>

## The hub is enforced

The planner refuses flows that bypass the hub. This is a correctness property, tested in `routing.test.ts`, not a convention:

```ts
// packages/routing/src/index.ts
if (purpose === "payout" && !onHub(req.source))
  throw new NoRouteError("payouts are funded from the customer's balance on the Stellar hub");
if (purpose === "collection" && !onHub(req.destination))
  throw new NoRouteError("collections settle into the Stellar hub");
```

See [ADR 0002](/decisions/0002-hub-enforced-by-planner).

## Priority flows

| Flow | Direction | Example | Payout rail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diaspora and B2B payouts into Africa | Inbound | EUR/GBP/USD → KES, XOF, TZS | Anchor's local bank or mobile money rail |
| Import payments to China and Hong Kong | Outbound | KES/GHS → CNY, HKD, USD | Supplier's Hong Kong or offshore USD/HKD account (onshore CNY conversion is restricted) |
| Import payments to India, UAE, Turkey, SE Asia | Outbound | KES → INR, AED, TRY, IDR | Local anchors |
| Payments to the Americas | Outbound | KES → USD, BRL, MXN | ACH/wire, Pix, SPEI |
| Intra-African trade | Both | KES ↔ GHS, XOF, TZS | Local anchors |

Corridors open in this order: **KES**, **XOF** and **TZS** first, then **NGN** through a licensed partner, then Ghana and South Africa. **None of these anchors is signed yet.**

## The naira exception

No licensed naira anchor is live on Stellar, so NGN sits in `OFF_STELLAR_PAYOUT_CURRENCIES` and routes over one CCTP hop to a partner that settles on Solana:

```text
ledger.reserve → cctp.transfer (Stellar → Solana) → partner.payout
```

When a Stellar naira anchor exists, removing NGN from that list moves the corridor onto the hub. The `cctp.transfer` leg itself [is not built](/integrations/cctp); on testnet, `bridge.simulated` stands in for it with the same bookings and a real Stellar leg.

## Regulation of outbound flows

Outbound FX from markets such as Nigeria is regulated: import payments need a Form M and must go through institutions licensed for FX. Corridor stays infrastructure. The licensed PSP or IMTO holds the FX permissions and the KYC; Corridor carries the compliance references (invoice number, Form M number) in the saga data and produces the reconciliation trail as audit evidence.
