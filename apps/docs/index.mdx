Corridor is the payment ledger and router that **licensed payment companies** run in their own environment, on their own keys. It runs cross-border stablecoin payouts across the anchors and providers a company already uses, **exactly once**, and keeps **one set of books that matches the chain**, from the customer's funding to the beneficiary's bank credit.

**Stellar is the settlement hub.** Corridor doesn't hold money, provide liquidity or carry licences — anchors and licensed operators do that, and Corridor runs payouts across them.

<Note>
Corridor is software, not a custodian or a money transmitter. It runs in the customer's environment and signs through the customer's own KMS or MPC ([Keys and signers](/architecture/keys-and-signers)). Customers keep their licences, their KYC, their funds and their end-customer relationships, and they start [read-only](/flows/read-only-pilot): no keys, nothing moves.
</Note>

## What runs today

A payout runs end to end on **Stellar testnet**: the route planner picks the legs, the saga engine funds the anchor order exactly once, and the double-entry ledger closes with no invariant violations.

Reproduce it with `pnpm stellar:wallets && pnpm stellar:smoke`, then `pnpm stellar:payout` for the whole saga. Both runs are on the [Evidence](/status/evidence) page with their transactions.

<Warning>
**No anchor is signed yet**, so no real local currency has moved on Stellar. SEP-6 collections are unimplemented, the `cctp.transfer` leg is declared in the route graph but not written, the MGUSD, PYUSD and USDT issuers are unset pending verification, and there is no treasury console. [Roadmap](/status/roadmap) says what is next; [Evidence](/status/evidence) says what has actually run.
</Warning>

## Who it's for

Licensed remittance operators, payout companies, PSPs and business-FX firms that send stablecoin payouts through **two or more anchors or providers**. That is where payouts stall halfway, retries pay twice and fees go unexplained. A company with one provider on one chain is well served by that provider's dashboard; Corridor is for the companies that have outgrown it.

## How it works

<Frame caption="Every flow is a collection into the Stellar hub or a payout out of it.">
  <img className="block dark:hidden" src="/images/diagrams/hub-and-spoke-light.svg" alt="Hub and spoke network with the Stellar hub at the centre" />
  <img className="hidden dark:block" src="/images/diagrams/hub-and-spoke-dark.svg" alt="Hub and spoke network with the Stellar hub at the centre" />
</Frame>

Every flow is either a **collection** into the Stellar hub or a **payout** out of it. A diaspora transfer into Nairobi is a euro collection followed by a shilling payout; a Nigerian importer paying a supplier in Shenzhen is a naira collection followed by a yuan payout.

Because anchors settle on Stellar itself, the common payout path has no bridge in it at all, and because every anchor speaks the same SEPs, adding a corridor is a domain and an asset rather than an integration: **N integrations instead of N×N corridors**.

Each payout is a durable saga. Every external step is recorded before Corridor waits on it, every movement is booked from the transaction that caused it, and the same 32-byte reference rides every leg.
