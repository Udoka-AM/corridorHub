## Context

In the prototype, every payout rail was a bespoke REST API: its own auth, its own order shape, its own status strings, its own webhook scheme. Each new region was an integration, and the integrations did not resemble each other.

Stellar anchors already have a common interface. SEP-1 publishes what an anchor serves, SEP-10 authenticates a vault against it, SEP-12 carries the customer records, SEP-38 gives a firm quote and SEP-31 is the cross-border payment itself. Anchors hold the licences and the local rails; they are the fiat spokes.

## Decision

**Every fiat spoke is reached through one partner interface, and `SepAnchor` implements that interface over the SEPs.**

```ts
interface RampPartner {
  rates(currency): Promise<Rates>;
  openPayout(req): Promise<PartnerOrder>;
  openCollection(req): Promise<PartnerOrder>;
  getOrder(id): Promise<PartnerOrder>;
  verifyWebhook(rawBody, headers, now?): boolean;
  parseWebhook(rawBody): PartnerOrder;
}
```

Every provider is normalised to `open → processing → completed | failed`, and a `PartnerRegistry` picks the partner per currency, direction and chain. A Stellar anchor and an off-Stellar partner are interchangeable to the legs.

## Consequences

- **Adding a corridor is configuration:** a home domain and an asset. That is what makes the spokes additive ([ADR 0002](/decisions/0002-hub-enforced-by-planner)).
- The SEP-10 challenge is verified **before** the vault signs it — it must be built by the anchor's own `SIGNING_KEY` and name this vault — so a stray endpoint cannot get the vault to sign something else. The JWT is cached for its lifetime.
- SEP-31 has no webhook. The sending anchor polls `GET /transactions/{id}`, which is what the saga's wait loop does, so `verifyWebhook` returns false and `parseWebhook` says so rather than pretending.
- The anchor assigns its own memo on the funding payment, so the Corridor Reference moves to the SEP-31 fields on that leg ([ADR 0003](/decisions/0003-reference-in-memo-hash)).
- **SEP-6 collections are not built.** `openCollection` checks that the anchor publishes a `TRANSFER_SERVER` and then raises, rather than guessing at a flow no signed anchor has yet exercised.
- Corridor carries no licence and holds no float. The anchor does both, in its own market.

## Alternatives rejected

- **One bespoke adapter per provider, as in the prototype:** N integrations that each have to be maintained, and no reason for the next one to be cheaper than the last.
- **A single aggregator in front of every market:** one counterparty risk, one price, and no way to use the anchor a customer already has a contract with.
